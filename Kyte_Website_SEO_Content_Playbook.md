# Kyte Website SEO and Content Playbook

October 2026 | Working brief for the website redesign

## Purpose and source of truth

Use this playbook to brief, draft, review, and publish copy for every page in the proposed Kyte website information architecture. It translates the October 2026 IA into page intent, content requirements, SEO decisions, and an evidence standard. It is a writing system, not finished public copy or a promise that any keyword will rank.

The October 2026 `Kyte_Website_IA.txt` controls the proposed navigation and routes. The older `Kyte_SEO_Copy_Guidelines.md` remains a useful voice and proof reference, but its four-service sitemap, keyword map, and rigid length targets predate the new two-cluster, ten-service IA. Resolve page structure against the IA before drafting. Treat all historic client results and team details as leads for verification, not approved public claims.

## 1. What the agency examples teach

The sites below were reviewed on 8 October 2026. These are editorial observations, not claims about their traffic, rankings, conversion rates, or the best design for Kyte.

| Agency | Observed pattern | What Kyte should use |
|---|---|---|
| Pineapple Design | Pairs a distinct point of view with selected work, client names, services, and outcome-led case stories. | Give the homepage one memorable position, then substantiate it with relevant work and clear service paths. |
| NetBramha | Makes research, strategy, and design discoverable; groups work by industries; puts named projects and outcomes close to the entry point. | Explain the method in buyer language and connect industry pages to real case evidence. |
| Metalab | Uses a very short proposition, then lets detailed product stories carry credibility. Its Pitch story names the brief, role, work, and results. | Keep top-level copy concise and let case studies explain complexity. |
| Goodface and its fintech offer | Organizes work by industry and product type. The fintech service page names buyer problems, specific deliverables, trust concerns, and relevant case work. | Make each industry page distinct by the buyer's problem, workflow, and proof, especially fintech. |
| Clay | Connects UX, brand, web, and development to named projects, while adding service explanations and useful questions. | Show how Kyte's capabilities work together without collapsing every service into generic agency language. |
| Ungrammary | Surfaces sector-specific case studies and practical buyer questions. | Use FAQs to resolve actual buying questions such as scope, input required, process, and handoff. |
| Onething | Pairs service and industry navigation with an active insights library covering design concepts, sector problems, and buyer comparisons. | Build insights around questions Kyte can answer from firsthand work, with clear paths into services and cases. |

Do not copy their headlines, claims, case results, or page structures word for word. Their scale and positioning differ from Kyte's. The shared lesson is a sequence: state the specific offer, explain who it helps, show the work, answer the buying questions, and give a clear next step.

## 2. Kyte positioning and voice

The site should present Kyte as a design-led agency that can connect product experience, websites, brand, and go-to-market creative. The UI/UX cluster should lead where the page is about digital product or website work. The branding and marketing cluster should remain substantial and specific, not appear as an afterthought.

Write to a founder, product lead, marketing lead, or business owner who is deciding whether Kyte understands their problem and can execute the scope. Start with the problem or outcome they recognize. Name the actual deliverable and how the team works. Use "you" where natural. Use plain English and a confident, conversational tone. No em dashes. Avoid empty phrases such as "transform your digital presence," "end-to-end solutions," "cutting-edge," and "seamless experiences" unless the sentence explains exactly what changes.

### Human and professional writing rules

These rules apply to all generated drafts, edits, titles, FAQs, case studies, and CTAs. Never use an em dash. Never use the formula "not X but Y," "not just X, but Y," or "this isn't about X, it's about Y." Do not create a run of clipped statements separated by full stops, such as "We listen. We design. We deliver." Connect related ideas in a natural sentence, and vary sentence length according to the thought. A short sentence is acceptable when it is the clearest complete answer, but do not use it as a recurring dramatic rhythm.

Avoid generic AI-sounding language: "unlock," "elevate," "reimagine," "leverage," "seamless," "game-changing," "transformative," and "in today's fast-paced world." Do not open every section with a rhetorical question, use neat three-part lists for effect, or write a claim that could fit any agency. Prefer an ordinary verb and a concrete object: "We redesigned the checkout flow" is stronger than "We elevated the commerce experience." Use contractions where they sound natural, and keep technical language only when the intended reader would use it. Read the page aloud; rewrite anything that sounds like a pitch deck, an SEO template, or a machine summarising a brief.

For each paragraph, ask: What would a Kyte team member say to this client in a real meeting? What specific work or evidence supports it? If the answer is unclear, gather a fact or remove the sentence. Do not mimic a competitor's voice. An AI-generated draft is a starting point and must receive a human editorial pass before publication.

Use an evidence ladder. Level 1 is a published case with approved name and documented result. Level 2 is an approved client example without a quantified result. Level 3 is a demonstrable process or sample deliverable. Level 4 is an unverified assertion. Publish Levels 1 to 3, and revise or remove Level 4. A strong claim format is: client or context + problem + Kyte's role + deliverable + observed outcome + date or measurement basis where relevant. Obtain client approval for sensitive or unpublished work.

Do not say Kyte does regulated compliance, security engineering, performance marketing, SEO execution, or app development unless the exact capability and scope are confirmed. Distinguish design from implementation, strategy from execution, and partner delivery from in-house delivery. Keep tool names, private prototypes, passwords, and internal workflows off public pages unless approved for disclosure.

## 3. SEO principles for the whole site

Every indexable page needs one primary reader intent and a distinct answer. Choose a primary phrase only after checking current search results, Search Console, and a fresh India keyword export. The IA's Ubersuggest volumes are a planning snapshot, not verified current demand. The phrase suggestions in Section 5 are hypotheses, not an approved keyword map.

Write a descriptive, concise page title; a clear H1; a useful first paragraph; and a page-specific meta description that describes the actual page. Google may rewrite displayed titles and snippets. Character counts are editing aids, not ranking rules. Use natural variants instead of repeating exact phrases in every heading. Add descriptive image alt text for meaningful images; use empty alt text for decorative images. Link with descriptive anchor text to the closest relevant service, industry, case, or article. Do not force a fixed number of links or FAQs.

Keep `/ui-ux-design-development` and `/branding-marketing` as service cluster landing pages. Their child pages must answer narrower buyer questions and avoid near-duplicate introductions. Industry pages should focus on sector problems and proof, not repeat the service catalog with a different keyword. The two Bangalore pages need genuinely local evidence, scope, and contact context; changing only the city name is insufficient. Do not publish location pages for places where Kyte cannot substantiate a meaningful offering.

Publish FAQs when they help a buyer. Do not write them solely for a rich-result tactic. Google has limited FAQ rich results largely to eligible government and health sites. Do not claim that omitting internal links makes a URL invisible to Google; old links, external links, and discovery through other routes can still matter.

**Keyword assignment rule:** one URL owns each intent cluster. The UI UX cluster or homepage may target the broad agency theme, the UI UX Bangalore page owns local agency selection, the website service page owns general website design, and the web design Bangalore page owns local website selection. The branding cluster covers the broad branding offer; the identity child explains strategic identity work. Assign variants to the same page where the underlying buyer question is the same. Recheck SERPs and Search Console before writing titles because several Ubersuggest intent labels and older stored metric dates are inconsistent with the visible result types.

## 4. Page brief required before drafting

Create one brief per URL with these fields: URL and page type; target reader and stage of decision; the one question the page must answer; primary phrase and two to four natural supporting concepts; SERP examples and date checked; unique angle against sibling pages; named proof available and approval state; scope and exclusions; CTA; internal links in and out; proposed title, H1, meta description, and image needs; author, reviewer, and publish status.

The draft should answer the user's question within the opening screen. Do not begin with a long agency introduction. Put proof near claims. Use section lengths that fit the material. A thin page padded to an arbitrary word count is worse than a short, complete page.

## 6. Reusable page structures

**Cluster page:** plain-language hero; who this cluster is for; service choices with clear differences; how the capabilities combine; three relevant work examples; process and collaboration; buying questions; contact CTA. Link every child page. Each cluster should have its own point of view and examples.

**Individual service page:** hero with service and outcome; situations that trigger the work; exact scope and deliverables; process in three to five stages; client inputs and handoff; one to three relevant cases or samples; boundaries; useful FAQs; CTA. Add pricing or timelines only when there is a real policy or range to quote. Do not force all eight sections from the older guideline if a service needs a different order.

**Industry page:** industry problem; why the experience or brand challenge is hard; how Kyte approaches it; specific services used; relevant cases; sector questions; CTA. Keep the language grounded in that sector's users and operations. If there is no approved sector proof, hold the page or label the evidence gap internally rather than publishing generic filler.

**Case study:** one sentence on the client and challenge; Kyte's role and constraints; starting state; research or diagnosis; key decisions with visual evidence; deliverables; observed outcomes with source and time window; what was learned; related service CTA. Attribute business outcomes carefully: describe association unless causal evidence exists. Do not use another agency's client results as Kyte proof.

**Insight article:** answer the exact query early; explain definitions or decision criteria; add original Kyte examples or practical tools; cite outside factual claims; show trade-offs; end with a useful next step and relevant internal links. Prioritise questions heard in discovery calls, research, and delivery. Articles should earn their place by helping a real visitor even without search traffic.

## 7. Drafting and review workflow

1. **Inventory proof.** Create an approved case list with client, sector, services, public permissions, source of metrics, testimonial permission, usable images, and contact owner. Record what cannot yet be published.
2. **Validate search intent.** Check current results and search data for each route. Assign one main intent per URL, record close variants, and identify overlap. Do not allow both a cluster and a child page to chase the same exact query with near-identical copy.
3. **Write the page brief.** Fill every field in Section 4. If the proof is missing, make the decision to collect it, narrow the claim, or defer the page.
4. **Draft for the reader.** Write the answer, differentiation, proof, and CTA first. Add search phrasing where it fits naturally. Do not generate a page by replacing the service name in a template.
5. **Edit and verify.** Review against this playbook, source material, approvals, scope boundaries, accessibility, and the live design. Have a subject expert review technical statements and the client owner review case claims.
6. **Publish and measure.** Check title, H1, meta, canonical, links, indexing, images, forms, and redirects. Track queries, clicks, leads, and page engagement over time. Revise based on evidence, not a fixed word-count rule.

## 8. AI copy generation brief

Copy the following into the writing tool with one completed page brief. Human review and claim verification remain mandatory.

> Draft copy for one Kyte website page using the supplied page brief and this playbook. Use the October 2026 IA for route and sibling-page boundaries. Write for the named buyer and answer the page's main question early. Keep the tone conversational, precise, and credible. Never use em dashes, the "not X but Y" or "not just X, but Y" format, or a series of clipped short sentences separated by full stops. Vary sentence length naturally and connect related thoughts. Avoid generic AI language, rhetorical openings, repetitive three-part phrasing, and claims that could fit any agency. Make every service claim match the supplied scope. Use only approved Kyte cases, numbers, testimonials, team facts, locations, and capabilities. If evidence is missing, insert `[VERIFY: what is needed]` in the working draft rather than inventing it. Provide: page goal, proposed title, meta description, H1, hero subhead, section headings and body copy, CTA labels, recommended internal links, image/alt brief, and a list of claims needing approval. Use the mapped keyword intent naturally without keyword stuffing or duplicate copy from sibling pages. Do not borrow competitor wording or publish verification placeholders. After drafting, run a separate human voice edit and identify any sentence that still sounds templated.

## 9. Launch and migration checks

Before launch, inventory all live Kyte URLs using the current site, sitemap, CMS exports, analytics, Search Console, and backlinks where available. Map each old URL to the closest relevant new URL. The live `/services` page needs a deliberate destination: a relevant service cluster or a new explanation may serve users better than redirecting to `/`. Map `/connect` to `/contact` if the latter replaces it. Include every live service, work, article, and campaign URL that changes. Test exact and wildcard rules after publishing; do not rely on the four proposed rules alone. Update navigation, footer, contextual links, canonical tags, XML sitemap, and campaign links to final URLs. Monitor 404s and Search Console after release.

## 10. Editorial acceptance checklist

- The page has a distinct buyer question and does not duplicate a sibling page.
- The opening states the offer, reader, or answer without filler.
- Every deliverable, result, client name, testimonial, and location has an approval source.
- The title, H1, description, links, and image text describe the real page.
- A relevant case or concrete process supports the page's main claim.
- The CTA says what happens next and works on mobile.
- A reviewer has checked technical scope, accessibility, and legal or privacy content where needed.
- The page has no em dashes, invented numbers, borrowed competitor language, or unpublished placeholders.
- The copy has no "not X but Y" formula, clipped sentence chain, canned rhetorical opener, or generic AI-sounding phrase.
- A human editor has read the page aloud and checked that the keyword fits naturally.
